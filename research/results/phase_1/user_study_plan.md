# Human User Study Plan

## Design

Compare two workflows:

- **Condition A, area-first:** participants begin with a conventional Dhaka
  neighborhood and use property filters; destination-based rank scores and
  DhakaNest explanations are absent.
- **Condition B, destination-first:** participants enter one to three important
  destinations, importance, commute limits, and housing needs, then use the
  full DhakaNest recommendation presentation.

Use a within-participant crossover design with matched but nonidentical rental
tasks. Counterbalance workflow order and task set, preferably with a balanced
Latin-square allocation. Freeze property snapshots and route evidence so both
conditions operate on comparable information. Record period/order effects.

## Participants and ethics

Eligible participants are consenting adults who can understand the study
language and plausibly make Dhaka rental decisions. Record rental-search
experience, Dhaka familiarity, and relevant accessibility needs. Exclude project
developers from the primary sample or report them separately.

Determine sample size before recruitment using simulation or an a priori power
analysis around a domain-justified smallest effect of practical interest. Phase
1 does not claim a participant count. Obtain institutional ethics approval when
required, informed consent, privacy safeguards, withdrawal procedures, and a
data-retention plan before collection. Do not collect unnecessary contact or
precise residential information.

## Tasks and procedure

1. Provide neutral training on both workflows using excluded examples.
2. Present a tenant scenario containing budget, housing needs, and recurring
   destinations. Area-first tasks also specify the profile's declared starting
   neighborhood.
3. Ask the participant to identify one preferred property and optionally a
   backup while thinking aloud only if that protocol is separately approved.
4. Record interactions, elapsed time, returned candidates, selected property,
   commute outcomes, and task completion.
5. Collect short post-task scales and a final comparative interview or survey.

Participants cannot see the independent relevance labels. Outcome assessors
cannot see workflow identity where blinding is technically possible.

## Outcomes

| Type | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Primary objective | Independent 0-3 relevance of the selected property |
| Objective supporting | Task completion, commute-limit satisfaction, time to decision, search actions, viewed properties, reversals |
| Self-reported supporting | Perceived usefulness, explanation usefulness, trust, satisfaction, workload/confidence |

Self-reported preference cannot replace objective suitability. Explanation
usefulness is measured only in the destination-first condition and therefore is
not a direct workflow effect unless both conditions receive matched explanatory
content in a separate study.

## Analysis

The participant-task is the observation, with dependence handled by participant
and task. Analyze selected-property relevance using a mixed-effects ordinal
model with workflow and period fixed effects and participant/task random effects
when estimable. Use suitable mixed models or paired robust alternatives for
time, actions, completion, and perception scales. Report effects and confidence
intervals, test order sensitivity, and retain incomplete-task/attrition counts.

Recruitment source, compensation, exclusions, missing-data policy, survey
instruments, task matching, power assumptions, and the analysis script must be
frozen before unblinding. No participants or responses were created in Phase 1.
